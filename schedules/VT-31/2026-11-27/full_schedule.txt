The Wise Owl and the Owlet
One crisp autumn morning, a wise owl took a young owlet out for one last flying lesson before Thanksgiving.
The little owl had been learning to fly for some time, but lately he had grown frustrated with his progress. He wanted to soar as smoothly as the older owls, to hold his course without wandering,
and to land as neatly as if the wind itself were guiding him.
Together, they lifted off from NAS Corpus Christi and climbed into the bright autumn sky. They completed their high work over Seagull, then followed the shining coastline north toward Palacios.
But the owlet struggled. Sometimes he let his altitude slip. Sometimes he drifted off heading. And when it came time to land, he could never quite settle on the center of the branch, no matter how
hard he tried.
“I suppose I’m not much of a wise owl yet,” the little one muttered.
The wise owl glided down and settled beside him.
“Do you remember when you were afraid to leave the nest?” he asked.
The owlet nodded.
“And when you could not find your way to Cabaniss without my help?”
The owlet nodded again.
The wise owl looked kindly at him.
“Well,” he said, “look where we are now.”
The little owl gazed out across Matagorda Bay. Below them, a stingray was splashing about in the shallow water.
“At least we’re doing better than him,” said the owlet.
“Be nice,” replied the wise owl. “He’s still learning to swim straight.”
The two owls chuckled. Then they spread their wings and began the journey home.
Somewhere along the way, the owlet realized that the coastline that had once seemed so strange had become a welcome sight. The bays, the beaches, and the familiar waters no longer made him
feel lost. They showed him how far he had come.
“Another flight tomorrow?” the owlet asked hopefully.
“After Thanksgiving,” replied the wise owl. “Even owls have families waiting for them.”
And together, beneath the soft autumn sky, they flew home to Corpus Christi.
